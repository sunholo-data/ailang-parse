\begin{abstract}
We demonstrate that deterministic parsing of LaTeX source preserves
structural fidelity that PDF-based OCR pipelines lose. We report
results on a 19-paper arXiv corpus across equations, tables,
citations, and bibliographies.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Scientific documents rely on structured notation that survives poorly
through rasterisation. When a paper is typeset to PDF and then OCR'd
back into text, equations become glyph soup and tables lose their
grid. Parsing the LaTeX source directly avoids this loss entirely.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A deterministic LaTeX parser producing a typed block ADT.
  \item Recursive \texttt{\textbackslash input} resolution with
        depth cap and cycle detection.
  \item Structural benchmarks against five PDF-based baselines.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method}

Let $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ denote an input token embedding and
$W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$ a projection matrix. The attention
weight for query $q$ and key $k$ is given by
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:attention}
  a(q, k) = \frac{\exp(q^\top k / \sqrt{d})}{\sum_{j} \exp(q^\top k_j / \sqrt{d})}.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:attention} is preserved verbatim as raw LaTeX
inside a \texttt{TextBlock} with \texttt{style="equation"}, which is
what downstream RAG pipelines actually want~\cite{vaswani2017}.

For a sequence of length $n$, the full attention matrix is
\begin{align}
  A &= \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right) V, \\
  Q &= XW_Q, \quad K = XW_K, \quad V = XW_V.
\end{align}

\section{Results}

Table~\ref{tab:results} shows structural coverage across the corpus.
Every PDF-based parser scores zero on equations because they emit
unstructured text; our parser emits typed equation blocks with the
LaTeX source preserved.

\centering

\hline
AILANG Parse       & 94\% & 79\% & 100\% \\
Docling (PDF-OCR)  & 88\% &  0\% &   0\% \\
MarkItDown (PDF)   & 71\% &  0\% &   0\% \\
Pandoc (on .tex)   & 79\% & 74\% &  89\% \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\end{table}

See \cite{devlin2019bert} and \cite{brown2020gpt3} for comparable
multi-file papers parsed end-to-end via recursive \texttt{\textbackslash input}
resolution.

\section{Conclusion}

Deterministic LaTeX parsing is not a marginal improvement over OCR
for scientific content --- it is a categorically different
capability. The source is the ground truth; rendering then re-parsing
the rendering discards information that cannot be recovered.
